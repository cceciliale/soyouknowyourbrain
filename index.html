Brain fact

Your brain doesn't “turn off” when you sleep.

Sleep changes brain activity rather than simply shutting it down. Different stages of sleep are associated with different patterns of brain activity and support processes involved in learning and memory.

01 • Sleep

What actually happens to your brain while you sleep?

Sleep is an active biological process. Getting enough sleep supports attention, learning, memory, and normal brain function. When you're sleep deprived, tasks that depend on attention, decision-making, learning, and remembering can become harder.

The interesting part: Sleep is not just “resting your brain.” Your brain is still active and processing information while you sleep.

02 • Anesthesia

Can anesthesia have effects after you wake up?

Yes, but the story is more complicated than “anesthesia damages your brain.” Many cognitive effects around anesthesia and surgery are temporary. Some patients — particularly older adults — can develop postoperative delirium, and some can experience cognitive problems that persist longer.

Why is this complicated? Anesthesia is only one part of the picture. Surgery, illness, inflammation, medications, sleep disruption, pain, and other factors can also affect cognition. Researchers continue to study exactly how these factors interact.

03 • Memory

Why did you walk into a room and forget why?

Sometimes the problem isn't that a memory disappeared — you may not have fully encoded the information in the first place. Attention helps determine what information gets processed and stored, while working memory helps you temporarily hold and manipulate information.

Try it: Next time you walk into a room, pause and notice what you were thinking about immediately beforehand.
